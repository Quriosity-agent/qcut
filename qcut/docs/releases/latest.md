# QCut v2026.11.11.1

### Fixed

- In a native export the picture no longer runs later than the sound after clips that are not a whole number of frames long. Each main-track clip was rounded up to whole frames on its own and the clips were joined end to end, so the picture of every clip after it moved later by the remainder while the sound stayed put: eleven such cuts left the end of a trailer 5 frames late. Every clip and gap is now cut to the frames and samples of its span on the timeline, counted from the timeline's start by the preview's frame rule, and the picture and the sound are joined from two lists, each giving every file its own stream's length.
- A mono source exports at the level it plays at. The preview and the in-browser export put a one-channel file into both channels at full level; FFmpeg's own conversion to stereo keeps the power instead and writes each channel at 1/√2. So every FFmpeg export of a mono clip came out 3.01 dB quieter than the preview and than the in-browser export of the same timeline: the native export the CLI uses, the Export dialog's FFmpeg export, the MP3 export, clip audio processed locally, and `compose` renders. Its compressors, gates, ducking and limiter also worked 3 dB lower than in the preview, and a track panned hard to one side came out 3 dB under. Every source is now made stereo first, a mono one at full level, in all of them; measured on a −21.08 dB mono tone, both engines write −21.08 dB in both channels (the native one wrote −24.09). Mono material therefore enters the mix 3 dB higher than before, the level the editor always played it at: an export is 3 dB louder from it where nothing holds it back, and less under a compressor or the limiter. Stereo sources are not lifted, and a one-channel export keeps its sources as they were.
- A clip whose in-point lies between two frames of its file starts on the frame on screen at that point, on the main track and as an overlay. Cuts made by time leave such in-points (the right half of a split, a ripple delete on bar lines), so does `timeline snap --to frames` when a trim takes up the difference, and so does any 30 fps file on a 24 fps timeline. FFmpeg drops the frame on screen at a seek point, so the clip began on the next one: its first frame was held for two, or the whole clip ran a frame ahead of the preview. Every output frame now shows the source frame on screen at its time, by the preview's rule (a frame that starts within 1 ms counts as on time); a clip that starts between two output frames is read from where its first frame falls, and its sound still starts at the in-point, to the sample. A source frame held for more than a quarter of a second before the in-point (a still stretch of a variable-rate recording) is still missed.
- Media overlays, text rasters and stickers keep their first frames when the clips under them come from files with different colour tags (a clip tagged `tv, bt709` after an untagged one). FFmpeg rebuilt the pass's filter graph at the change, and every overlay that appeared after it started 2–5 frames into itself, with its last frame held to make up the length. The graph is now kept, and the picture is read with one set of tags throughout.
- A native export has one colour description: BT.709, limited range, on the file and on every frame, as the in-browser export writes it. Each clip used to keep whatever its file said, a PNG or JPEG still was written BT.601 with no tags at all, and so was a recording with a drawn cursor. A timeline that mixed them came out as one file that changed description from clip to clip under its first clip's tags, and a player showed the other clips in shifted colours where the preview and the in-browser export showed them right. Worst error on colour bars (8-bit levels), before → after: a BT.601 clip 40 → 4, an untagged SD clip 40 → 4, a PNG 40 → 3, a JPEG 40 → 4, an untagged HD overlay 24 → 3; a full-range clip stayed full range inside a file tagged limited. A source is now read the way the preview reads it (by its own tags; one that has none as BT.601 below 720 lines and BT.709 from there up, limited range) and converted once, where its clip is encoded; a BT.709 clip passes through bit for bit. An untagged file whose coding does not match its size still comes out shifted, as it shows in the preview. An HDR source (PQ, HLG) is left as it was.
- A clip with frame interpolation (blend or motion-compensated) reaches its last source frame in a native export. Its last output frame or two used to hold the blend before that frame whenever the frame did not fall exactly on an output frame: the interpolation was given the length of two output frames to look ahead, which is one frame of a 12 fps source on a 24 fps timeline, and it needs two of the clip's own.
- A transition drawn by the local runtime (the 剪映 catalogue, in the in-browser export and in previews) no longer comes out with frames of the wrong moment. The engine draws on a Metal queue of its own, and the bridge read each frame back as soon as the seek returned: what it got was whatever the output texture's pooled storage held, one of the two clips alone, an earlier frame of the transition, or half of this one. It showed while another job kept the GPU busy: with a render running in another app, 20 to 30% of the frames of a dissolve were wrong (141 of 552 frames in 23 renders), where a quiet machine, or one with only its CPU busy, gave none in 480. The bridge now waits for the engine's renderer to finish before it reads, and 432 frames in 18 renders beside the same render were all the right ones. A bridge built before this is no longer used wherever it is found: the app asks each one whether it waits, and a release cannot be packaged with one that does not.
- An overlay no longer darkens the whole export. With one overlay anywhere on the timeline every frame went from YUV to RGBA and back with FFmpeg's default rounding, which lowered the picture's luma by 1.2–1.6 levels (8-bit) in the export this was measured on. The picture now stays in its own format and each overlay is converted to it; a layer with a blend mode is blended in RGB with exact rounding.
- An overlay that is not cropped, masked, cut out or faded is no longer filtered pixel by pixel. Every overlay went through two `geq` passes whether it needed them or not: eight plain overlays over a 30 s, 1470×630 picture took 13.9 s to draw, and now take 2.1 s.
- `editor:timeline:apply --replace` gives the project's main track to the manifest track marked `"isMain": true`, as `timeline export` marks it, and `--verify` checks that it reads back as the main one. The first media track of the manifest used to take it, so a timeline exported with an overlay track above its main track came back with the overlay track as the main one. An unmarked manifest still gives it to its first media track; more than one mark, or a mark on a track that is not a media track, is refused before anything changes.
- A `bitrate` the export cannot read is refused with the forms it takes (`"24Mbps"`, `"24M"`, `"8000kbps"`, `"8000k"`). A bare number or a string without its unit used to export at 8 Mbps without a word.
- An export without `--preset` takes the bitrate of the size it exports. A `width` and `height` given with the request kept the bitrate of the project's own size.
- A sticker on a hidden track, and a hidden sticker, are no longer drawn by the native export.
- `editor:timeline:info` and `editor:timeline:export` without `--project-id` read the project the editor has open, as the `track`, `mix` and `element` commands do. They answered `Missing --project-id`.

### Added

- `editor:editing:insert-gap --at <seconds> --duration <seconds>` opens empty time, the reverse of `delete-range`: clips from the point on move later, a clip the point falls inside is split there, and level and pan automation opens with them (bus and master curves too when every unlocked track moved; `--keep-automation` leaves them). Every unlocked track by default, or the tracks named with `--track-id`; one undo step. Making room for a title sequence used to mean exporting the timeline, adding the seconds to every clip in a script and applying the whole thing back.
- `editor:timeline:snap --to frames` puts clip edges on whole frames without moving what the clips show: the trim on each side takes up the difference, and two clips that meet at a cut keep meeting. An edge with no unused source to move into goes to the frame on its inner side; a cut with none on either side, a clip with a speed curve or a freeze frame, and a clip that would be left shorter than a frame stay and are listed in `skipped` with the reason. Visual clips by default (`--frame-align all` snaps sound too), narrowed with `--track-id` and `--element-ids`; `--dry-run` reports the changes without making them.
- `--snap-frames` on `editing delete-range`, `editing insert-gap`, `timeline split`, `timeline move` and `timeline trim` rounds the command's times to the nearest frame of the project's rate, and the answer carries the times that were used as `snapped`.
- `editor:timeline:export` reports `frameAlignment`: how many clips start or end between frames, how many of those are on the main track, and the first twenty of them.
- `editor:media:replace --media-id <id> --source <file>` puts a newly rendered file in a media item's place: the file is imported, every clip that played the old item plays the new one in one undo step (place, length, trims and settings kept), and the old item is removed. A clip that cannot take the new file refuses the whole replace, and the import is taken back out. It sits in the confirm tier (`--force`).
- `editor:export:start --exclude-tracks <id,…>` exports without the named tracks, picture and sound, and leaves the project as it is; the engine is chosen from the tracks that are left. Native exports only.
- `editor:export:start --keep-temp` keeps the export's intermediate files and writes every FFmpeg command it ran, whole, to `ffmpeg-commands.sh` beside them; the job reports the folder as `intermediates`. The log line of the media overlay pass also holds its whole command now, inputs included, where it had only the filter.
- A native export checks its own length and colour description. A joined picture or a finished file with another number of frames than the timeline gives it, a filter graph FFmpeg rebuilt part-way through a pass that reads several inputs, and a finished file that does not say BT.709, limited range, are named in the job's `warnings`, with the clips or gaps that came out another length than planned.
